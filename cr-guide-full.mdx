# Responsibilities of CRs and Their Experiences So Far

Being a Class Representative (CR) at UVCE is one of the most impactful roles you can take. This guide walks you through what the role actually entails, straight from seniors who've lived it.

<Callout type="info" title="What is a CR?">
A Class Representative is the communication link between your class and the college administration. Whatever information the HOD or faculty members need to share with your class, you're responsible for getting it to everyone.
</Callout>

---

## Core Responsibilities of a CR

### The Main Duty: Be the Communication Bridge

**The main thing we do in our branch is we are the connection link between the department and our class.** Whatever information the HOD or faculty members want to communicate, we are responsible for sharing it with the entire class.

**Your key responsibilities:**
- Contact the respective faculty members according to their allotted timetable
- Inform or call them when required by the college
- Relay official notices, exam schedules, and important deadlines
- Send/forward information from COE, HOD, or faculties to students
- Be the first point of contact when something needs to reach your class

<Callout type="warning" title="That's Pretty Much It">
Apart from these, we haven't really had many other responsibilities as CRs in our EEE department. I think it will be mostly the same in CSE as well.
</Callout>

---

## The Myth vs Reality

### Don't Believe the Scary Stories

**People may scare u saying being a CR is risky, but honestly it's not that big of a deal. It's mainly just about communication only.**

The role isn't complicated. The challenge isn't the work itself—it's something more subtle.

---

## The Real Challenge: Coordination With Your Co-CR

### This is Where It Gets Hard

**See when you are a CR, your co-CR has to be responsible as well.** If you guys don't have coordination it gets to your nerves and you burnout.

**How some seniors are handling it:**

Me and our co-CR are like, 1 whole semester he'll single handedly handle, next sem I'll handle. There's no coordination. It's just division. Some sems are more stressful some are less and this one is eating me up.

**The reality:** Your workload isn't constant. Some semesters are heavier. The trick is coordinating with your co-CR so neither of you burns out.

---

## What This Role Actually Does to You

### Personal Growth Through Pressure

**Not sad, it's fun, it's breaking me everyday, I'm rebuilding it bit by bit.** That's why this CR thing is about—**it makes you more confident, responsible and resilient**.

You'll experience:
- **More confidence** → Communicating with faculty, HOD, entire class
- **More responsibility** → People depend on you for critical information  
- **More resilience** → Handling stress, learning to manage work, adapting

## The Bottom Line

Being a CR is fundamentally about **responsibility and communication**. It's not glamorous, it's not always smooth, but it builds you into someone more capable, more aware, and more resilient.

Your class doesn't need a perfect CR. They need someone who shows up and communicates clearly.

<Callout type="info" title="Before You Take the Role">
Ask yourself: **Can I be the reliable person my class needs to count on?**

If yes, then this role will transform you in ways you won't expect until you're already in it.
</Callout>
